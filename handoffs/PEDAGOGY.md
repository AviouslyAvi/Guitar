# Pedagogy — 3-week fretboard memorization

Conceptual ground for every lesson. Read this in any chat that writes lesson copy or designs a drill. Sourced from synthesis of public guitar-learning material; **none of the wording below is lifted from any source — it's a distillation, safe to use as a base for original prose.**

## The two engines (do both, every day)

1. **Anchor notes.** Open / 3 / 5 / 7 / 12 on each string. Memorize these cold; everything else is half-step inference.
2. **Octave shapes.** Two patterns:
   - "Two strings down, two frets up" — for E↔D and A↔G string pairs.
   - "Two strings down, three frets up" — when string 3 (G→B) is involved, because G→B is a major third (3 frets), not a fourth.
   Once octaves are automatic, the neck halves in size.

## Daily drill protocol — 15–20 min

Frequency beats duration. Three 7-min sessions a week is worse than 15 min daily.

- **Single-string call-outs (warmup, ~5 min):** pick a string, climb 0→12 saying notes aloud, then climb back down. Different string each session.
- **Note-of-the-day (focus, ~10 min):** pick one natural note (say, F). Find every F on the neck, ascending and descending, all six strings.
- **Vertical drill (integration, ~5 min):** pick a note, hit every octave from low to high without pausing. Drills the octave shapes into reflex.

## Week shape

| Week | Strings | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 & 5 | Anchors + first octave shape. Root strings — every barre chord and CAGED root sits here. Pays back fastest. |
| 2 | 4 & 3 | Cross-check against Week 1. **String 3 is the odd one out** (G→B is a major third) — call this out explicitly; it breaks the pattern. |
| 3 | 2 & 1 + integration | Strings 2 & 1 reuse Week 1/2 patterns offset by string 3's quirk. End with the "name every landing note while soloing" gate test. |

## Tips & tricks worth folding into lessons

- **Say notes aloud.** Sub-vocalizing is meaningfully slower than speaking.
- **Practice descending too.** Most players only drill ascending and freeze going the other way.
- **Use CAGED chord roots as a check.** If you can't find the root of an A-shape barre at fret 7, you don't really know string 5.
- **The 12th fret is a free win** — it's the open string an octave up. Plant that early.
- **Naturals first, accidentals second.** Don't drill sharps/flats until naturals are reflexive — sharps are just half-step inference from naturals.
- **Honest progress.** Skips and early stops shouldn't pad accuracy. Streak credit gates at ≥10 answers (existing rule). Reading lessons doesn't extend the streak.
- **The work/home split exists for a reason.** Drill defaults to "work" session (focused). Don't lose the storage shape.
- **Octave facts to memorize:**
  - 6→4 and 5→3: +2 frets
  - 4→2 and 3→1: +3 frets (B-string offset)
  - Adjacent-string unison shift: 5 frets back, except 3→2 is 4 frets back
